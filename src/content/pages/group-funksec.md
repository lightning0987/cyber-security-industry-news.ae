## FunkSec is an AI-assisted ransomware-as-a-service group with no recorded aliases

FunkSec runs a ransomware-as-a-service model in which affiliates use AI tooling to lower the technical skill needed for attacks. The group opened its data leak site in December 2024 and was added to this tracker that month. Its profile here lists no aliases, tools or techniques, so this page draws only on its UAE claims. All figures come from leak-site monitoring, with a snapshot dated 24 November 2026.

## FunkSec's UAE record is a short episode in two sectors

FunkSec ranks tenth on this tracker, behind LockBit 3.0, which leads with 10 claims. FunkSec's UAE claims span [Government and Defense](/uae-ransomware-tracker/government-and-defense/) and Technology, and they fall in [2024](/uae-ransomware-tracker/2024/) and [2025](/uae-ransomware-tracker/2025/). The first claim is dated 24 December 2024 and the most recent 19 February 2025. The snapshot shows no return since, so FunkSec reads as a single episode rather than a recurring campaign.

## What this means for UAE organisations

1. Map your suppliers' access to government data. DESC ISR v3 reaches key suppliers handling government data, and FunkSec's claims sit in Government and Defense and Technology.
2. Confirm whether a designating authority has placed your organisation in scope of UAE IAS v2. If so, check your status against its 39 Priority One controls, which are mandatory for everything in scope.
3. Prepare evidence across the 13 DESC ISR v3 control domains if you serve Dubai government. Assessors expect documented proof, not statements of intent.
4. Keep monitoring for FunkSec activity despite the quiet period since February 2025. An affiliate model means the group can resume claims without any visible warning.
5. Verify any FunkSec claim against your own logs before treating it as a breach. If personal data is confirmed affected, follow the UAE PDPL breach notification duties.
