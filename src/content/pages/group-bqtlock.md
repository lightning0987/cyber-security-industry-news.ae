## BQTLock is a ransomware-as-a-service operation tied to hacktivist networks

BQTLock runs as a ransomware-as-a-service scheme that the tracker added to its source profiles on 31 July 2025. The profile describes file encryption with AES-256 and RSA-4096, with payment demanded in Monero. The same profile links BQTLock to pro-Palestinian hacktivist networks. It also describes wave-based campaigns that give victims 48 hours to respond.

## BQTLock's UAE record is one dated episode, not a sustained campaign

BQTLock ranks 14th on this tracker with two claimed UAE organisations. LockBit 3.0, the most active group, has 10. Leak-site monitoring, as of the 28 November 2026 snapshot, places both BQTLock claims on 11 October 2025. The claims are spread across [Retail and E-Commerce](/uae-ransomware-tracker/retail-ecommerce/) and [Education](/uae-ransomware-tracker/education/), so BQTLock shows no sector concentration. BQTLock has no claims dated 2026, which fits a single wave rather than a group that returns.

## What this means for UAE organisations

1. Rehearse a 48-hour decision cycle. BQTLock's profile describes short ransom deadlines, so name who can authorise isolation, legal advice and external communications outside working hours.
2. Keep offline copies of critical data and test restores. BQTLock's AES-256 and RSA-4096 encryption makes recovery without the attacker's key unrealistic.
3. Map where personal data is held in retail, e-commerce and education systems. The UAE Personal Data Protection Law has federal scope and breach notification duties, so an organisation needs to establish quickly what a claim could affect.
4. Verify every claim naming your organisation before acting on it. A leak-site entry is an assertion by BQTLock, not a confirmed breach.
5. Raise monitoring of internet-facing services during periods of regional political tension. BQTLock's profile ties the group to hacktivist networks, so timing may follow events rather than sector.
