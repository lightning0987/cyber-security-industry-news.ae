## KillSec is a former hacktivist group running ransomware-as-a-service

KillSec began as a hacktivist group aligned with the Anonymous movement. KillSec moved into ransomware in October 2023 and launched a ransomware-as-a-service (RaaS) platform in June 2024. The platform offers affiliates an 88% revenue split, so different operators may run KillSec attacks. The profile names healthcare, financial services and government as primary targets, with over 250 victims by late 2025.

## KillSec's UAE record is two claims across two sectors

KillSec ranks seventeenth on this tracker, with two claimed UAE organisations in leak-site monitoring as of 2026-11-28. LockBit 3.0, the most active group on the tracker, has 10 claims. KillSec's claims fall one each in [Financial Services](/uae-ransomware-tracker/financial-services/) and [Healthcare](/uae-ransomware-tracker/healthcare/), matching two of its stated primary sectors. Neither claim falls in [2026](/uae-ransomware-tracker/2026/), so KillSec reads as a sporadic presence, not a sustained campaign.

## What this means for UAE organisations

1. Confirm whether your firm is authorised in the DIFC. DFSA TRM applies inside that free zone, and KillSec has one claimed Financial Services organisation.
2. Rehearse a PDPL breach notification decision triggered by a leak-site claim. UAE PDPL has federal scope and carries breach notification duties. Healthcare organisations hold personal data, and KillSec has one claimed Healthcare organisation.
3. Assign an owner to check leak-site listings for your organisation name and domains. Verify each listing against internal logs, because a claim is an attacker's assertion, not a confirmed breach.
4. Test defences against a range of intrusion routes, not one playbook. KillSec's affiliate model means different operators may use different methods, and the group profile lists no tools or techniques.
