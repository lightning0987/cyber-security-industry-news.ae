## APT73 is a self-styled APT group that surfaced in April 2024

APT73 is a ransomware group reportedly first seen in mid-April 2024, and its source profile dates its listing to 22 April 2024. The operators reportedly branded themselves an advanced persistent threat, a label normally associated with state-linked actors. Early public information about APT73 reportedly came largely from LockBit, a rival ransomware group. APT73 is also tracked under the alias bashe.

## APT73's UAE record is a single episode on 27 April 2026

APT73 ranks eleventh on this tracker, with two UAE claims recorded from leak-site monitoring (snapshot 2026-11-23). LockBit 3.0 leads the comparison with 10 claims. Both APT73 claims carry the date 27 April 2026, one in [Government and Defense](/uae-ransomware-tracker/government-and-defense/) and one in [Energy and Utilities](/uae-ransomware-tracker/energy-and-utilities/). APT73 logged no UAE claims in 2024 or 2025, and nothing has followed since the single burst in [2026](/uae-ransomware-tracker/2026/).

## What this means for UAE organisations

1. Check whether your organisation, or a supplier holding your data, operates in Government and Defense or Energy and Utilities. Those are the two sectors where APT73 made its claims.
2. Map your controls to UAE IAS v2 if you are in scope. Its 39 Priority One controls are mandatory, and an assessor will expect evidence for each. Do the same against the 13 control domains of DESC ISR v3 if Dubai government work places you in scope.
3. Hunt on behaviours rather than group-specific indicators. The source profile lists no tools or techniques for APT73, and its APT label is self-declared.
4. Rehearse your UAE PDPL breach notification process, which applies to any organisation processing personal data of individuals in the UAE. Treat a leak-site claim as a trigger for verification, not as a confirmed breach.
