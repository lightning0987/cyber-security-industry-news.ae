## APT73 is a self-styled APT group that surfaced in April 2024

APT73, also listed under the alias bashe, is a ransomware group that emerged in mid-April 2024, according to its source profile. APT73 reportedly calls itself an advanced persistent threat, but that label is self-applied and unverified. Early public information about APT73 reportedly came from LockBit, so the group's profile rests on second-hand reporting.

## APT73's UAE record is a single day, not a campaign

APT73 ranks eleventh on this tracker with two claimed UAE organisations (leak-site monitoring, snapshot 2026-11-25). LockBit 3.0, the most active group used for comparison, has 10 claims. Both APT73 claims carry the date 2026-04-27 and fall in different sectors: [Government and Defense](/uae-ransomware-tracker/government-and-defense/) and [Energy and Utilities](/uae-ransomware-tracker/energy-and-utilities/). APT73 has no UAE claims before [2026](/uae-ransomware-tracker/2026/), although it emerged in 2024.

## What this means for UAE organisations

1. Confirm with your designating authority whether UAE IAS v2 or DESC ISR v3 applies to you. Sector labels are only a proxy for scope, and APT73 has claimed organisations in two sectors those frameworks usually cover.
2. Check your ransomware resilience against the 39 Priority One controls of UAE IAS v2. Those controls are mandatory for everything in scope, so list any gaps first.
3. Gather incident response and backup evidence under the 13 control domains of DESC ISR v3. A framework aligned with ISO 27001 and NIST CSF typically calls for documented, tested procedures.
4. Define in advance who decides whether a leak-site claim amounts to a notifiable breach under the UAE PDPL. The law carries breach notification duties for any organisation processing personal data of individuals in the UAE.
5. Add APT73 and the alias bashe to your threat-intelligence watchlists. Treat any new APT73 claim as an attacker assertion until your own investigation confirms or rules it out.
