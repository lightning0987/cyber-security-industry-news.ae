## What happened

[Hospitality](/uae-ransomware-tracker/hospitality/) recorded no ransomware claims against UAE
organisations across 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 or 2025. The sector's record was empty for the
whole of that span.

Three claims arrived in 2026. The first was recorded on 4 May 2026 and the most recent on
5 August 2026. DragonForce, Everest and Lamashtu account for one claim each.

## Why three separate groups matters

A sector moving from zero to 3 claims could mean one operation found a viable route and reused
it. That is not what this record shows. Three different groups published one claim each inside
four months.

Three independent entrants is a different signal from one repeat offender. It argues that something changed about the sector's
exposure rather than about any one attacker's interest. Three groups do not coordinate a
campaign by publishing once each.

The alternative reading is that three claims is simply too small a sample to carry a signal at
all. Both readings are available. The honest position is that the sector is worth watching, not
that a trend has been established.

## Where Hospitality sits on the tracker

Hospitality ranks eleventh of 13 UAE sectors with 3 claimed organisations, well below the
average of 8.3 claims per active sector. It carries 2.8% of all attributed UAE claims.

Rank understates the change. Every other sector with claims in 2026 also had claims before it.
Hospitality is the only sector on this tracker whose entire record falls inside one partial year.

[Retail and E-Commerce](/uae-ransomware-tracker/retail-ecommerce/) shows the closest comparable
pattern. That sector ran at 1 claim a year through 2024, then recorded 4 in 2025 and 3 in 2026.
It did not start from zero, but it changed gear at a similar point.

## What the data cannot tell you

A sector with an empty record is not a sector that was never attacked. Organisations that pay before publication never appear
in leak-site data at all. Coverage of leak sites was also thinner in earlier years.

The honest statement is narrower than it looks. Hospitality has no published claims before 2026
in this dataset. Whether it had incidents is a different question, and this data does not answer
it. The [methodology page](/methodology/) sets out that limitation in full.

## What the sector holds that attackers want

Hospitality organisations hold guest identity documents, payment credentials and booking
histories. They hold them for people who are transient, which makes notification harder than it
is for a customer base with standing accounts.

The operational profile compounds it. Property management systems, point of sale terminals and
door access often share an administrative boundary. Franchise and management-contract partners
frequently sit inside that boundary without sitting inside the brand's access reviews.

Neither observation comes from this dataset. Both are worth stating because the dataset shows
arrival and says nothing about route.

## What this means for UAE organisations

1. Read the sector as newly active rather than lightly targeted, because an empty record before
   2026 is a statement about publication, not about safety.
2. Map your obligations under the [UAE PDPL](/guides/uae-pdpl-breach-notification/), which
   covers guest identity, payment and booking records.
3. Check that property management and point of sale systems do not share administrative
   credentials with corporate systems.
4. Confirm that franchise and management-contract partners sit inside your access review, since
   hospitality brands often operate through third parties.
5. Establish how a property checks guests in without its systems, because that answer decides
   what an outage actually costs.
6. Watch the sector page as 2026 completes, since three claims in a partial year set no baseline.

## Summary

Hospitality recorded its first UAE ransomware claims in 2026, three of them between May and
August, from three separate groups. The sector had an empty record for the six years before.
Claims are assertions published by attackers rather than confirmed breaches, and a small sample
supports watching rather than concluding.
