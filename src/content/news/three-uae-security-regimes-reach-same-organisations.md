## The overlap is structural, not accidental

Three separate regimes can reach one organisation in the United Arab Emirates at the same time.
Each was written by a different authority for a different purpose.

[DESC ISR v3](/guides/desc-isr-compliance-dubai/) is issued by the Dubai Electronic Security
Center and applies in the emirate of Dubai. It sets 13 control domains.

[UAE IAS v2](/guides/uae-ias-nesa-controls/) is issued federally by the Signals Intelligence
Agency. It applies across all seven emirates to critical national infrastructure and government
entities. It sets 188 controls, of which 39 carry Priority One status.

The [UAE PDPL](/guides/uae-pdpl-breach-notification/) is Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 and
governs personal data across the country, supervised by the UAE Data Office.

## How an organisation ends up inside all three

A Dubai entity operating infrastructure that matters nationally, and processing personal data,
meets the scope test of each one separately.

It falls under ISR v3 because of where it is. It falls under IAS v2 because of what it runs.
It falls under the PDPL because of what it processes. None of those tests references the others.

Suppliers reach the same position by a different route. ISR v3 extends to key suppliers handling Dubai
government data. That obligation arrives through a contract clause rather than through the
regulation directly.

## Where they converge and where they do not

The control content converges substantially. Access management, cryptography, logging, incident
response and supplier assurance appear in both ISR v3 and IAS v2. Much of the same work also
satisfies the PDPL requirement for appropriate technical measures.

The filings do not converge at all. Each regime has its own assessment process, its own
supervisor and its own evidence expectations. A single control set can serve all three. A single
evidence pack cannot.

Free zones add a fourth path. Firms registered in the DIFC or ADGM sit under
[their own data protection regimes](/guides/difc-adgm-data-protection-comparison/) rather than
the federal PDPL. Supervisors and penalty ceilings differ.

## What the ransomware data shows about scope

[Government and Defense](/uae-ransomware-tracker/government-defense/) is the second most claimed
UAE sector on this tracker with 13 claimed organisations.
[Financial Services](/uae-ransomware-tracker/financial-services/) carries 11 and
[Energy and Utilities](/uae-ransomware-tracker/energy-utilities/) carries 4.

Those three sectors cover most of the population that sits inside more than one regime at once.
The combined figure is small against the site total of 108. That is the point worth making.
Regulatory exposure is not proportional to claim volume, and a sector with few claims can carry
the heaviest consequence per incident.

## The cost of treating them as one programme

Organisations usually discover the overlap late, during a first assessment, and respond by
building a single combined programme. That works for the controls and fails for the evidence.

Each supervisor asks for artefacts in its own form. A combined evidence pack has to be disassembled under
time pressure, once per assessment. That is exactly when the work is hardest to do well.

The cheaper sequence is the opposite. Build one control set deliberately, then produce the evidence
packs separately from it. Each assessment then draws from a common source without sharing a
format.

## What this means for UAE organisations

1. Test scope against each regime separately, because none of the three scope tests references
   the others.
2. Build one control set across all applicable regimes, since the technical content converges.
3. Build separate evidence packs, because the assessment processes do not.
4. Check contracts for flowed-down obligations, which is how most private firms enter ISR scope.
5. Confirm whether any group entity is registered in the DIFC or ADGM, since that changes the
   data protection regime entirely.
6. Keep dated test results, because every regime here judges the appropriateness of technical
   measures after an incident rather than before.

## Summary

DESC ISR v3, UAE IAS v2 and the UAE PDPL can reach one organisation simultaneously through three
unrelated scope tests. Their control requirements overlap and their evidence requirements do not.

authority.
