## What happened

APT73 published both of its UAE claims on 27 April 2026. The group has no other record against
a UAE organisation in this dataset, in any period.

It is the only group on this tracker whose entire record falls on a single date. Two entries
published together point to one publication event rather than to sustained activity.

## Who is affected in the UAE

[Government and Defense](/uae-ransomware-tracker/government-defense/) and
[Energy and Utilities](/uae-ransomware-tracker/energy-utilities/) account for 1 claim each.

Both sectors sit inside the heaviest regulatory scope in the United Arab Emirates. Energy and
Utilities holds 4 claims in total on this tracker, so a single entry represents a quarter of
that sector's record.

## How this compares

APT73 ranks eleventh of 46 groups on a record of 2 claims. That ranking says more about the tail of this dataset
than about the group. Twenty-four of the 46 groups have claimed exactly one UAE organisation.

A same-day pair carries less signal than two claims spread across months. It is one event, not
a pattern.

## What to do

1. Confirm your designation as critical national infrastructure with the authority that makes
   it, rather than inferring it from your industry.
2. Complete the 39 Priority One controls in [UAE IAS v2](/guides/uae-ias-nesa-controls/) first,
   because the standard makes them mandatory without tailoring.
3. Check whether [DESC ISR v3](/guides/desc-isr-compliance-dubai/) also reaches you through a
   Dubai government contract, which brings 13 control domains with it.
4. Keep operational technology networks separate from corporate networks, and prove the
   separation rather than asserting it.
5. Record how a control system runs without its management layer, because that is the first
   question an incident asks.
